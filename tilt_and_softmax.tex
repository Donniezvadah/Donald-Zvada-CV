\section{What the sampler needs}

Each sweep draws the latent path $\bs S=(S_{it})$ by forward filtering,
backward sampling, then draws the slot count $D$ from its full conditional
given $\bs S$, then redraws $\bs\pi^{(0)}$ and the transition rows from
their Dirichlet posteriors, then updates the hurdle--Dirichlet emission
parameters. The order of the middle pair is required: $D$ is drawn with
$\bs\pi^{(0)}$ and $\bGamma$ \emph{integrated out}, and those matrices are
redrawn only afterwards.

Write $\Dplus=\lvert\{d:\exists(i,t),\,S_{it}=d\}\rvert$ for the number of
states occupied anywhere on the path, including unobserved visits, and
relabel those states to $\{1,\dots,\Dplus\}$. Candidates for the next $D$
are the integers
\[
D'\in\{\Dplus,\dots,\Dmax\}.
\]
Bayes' rule on the relabelling class $[\bs S]$ is
\begin{equation}
\Pi\bigl(D=D'\mid[\bs S]\bigr)
\ \propto\
p_D(D')\,\frac{D'!}{(D'-\Dplus)!}\,
\Prb(\bs S\mid D').
\label{eq:bayes}
\end{equation}
The combinatorial prefactor counts injections of the occupied labels into
$\{1,\dots,D'\}$. The whole update is exact (acceptance probability one)
if and only if $\Prb(\bs S\mid D')$ can be evaluated as a function of the
path counts, $D'$, and the fixed hyperparameters. That is the quantity a
softmax transition does not supply.

\section{The two tilt functions}

The prior on cardinality is Poisson$(\lambda)$ truncated to
$\{1,\dots,\Dmax\}$. Given $D$, the initial distribution and every
transition row are Dirichlet, with concentrations that depend on $D$
through two scalar maps.

\begin{definition}[GMFM tilt and persistence shift]
\label{def:tilts}
For hyperparameters $\alpha_0>0$, $\beta\in[0,1)$, and target stay
probability $\varsigma\in(0,1)$,
\begin{equation}
\gammaD=\alpha_0 D^{-\beta},
\qquad
\kapD=\max\Bigl\{\frac{D\varsigma-1}{1-\varsigma},\,0\Bigr\}.
\label{eq:tilts}
\end{equation}
The initial law is $\bs\pi^{(0)}\mid D\sim\Dir_D(\gammaD\one_D)$. Row $k$
of a transition matrix (shared, or participant-specific $\bGamma^{(i)}$)
is
\begin{equation}
\bGamma_k\mid D\sim\Dir_D(\bs\eta_k),
\qquad
\eta_{kl}=\gammaD\bigl(1+\kapD\,\indic{k=l}\bigr),
\qquad
\etadot=\gammaD(D+\kapD).
\label{eq:eta}
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

The first map is the mixture-of-finite-mixtures tilt of
Fr\"uhwirth-Schnatter, Malsiner-Walli and Gr\"un: $\beta=0$ is a static
Dirichlet, $\beta=1$ is the dynamic case. The second map is the unique
diagonal shift, inside this one-parameter family, that holds the prior mean
stay probability fixed across candidate cardinalities.

\begin{proposition}[Prior persistence]
\label{prop:stay}
For every $k$,
$\E[\Gamma_{kk}\mid D]=(1+\kapD)/(D+\kapD)$.
If $\kapD\equiv\kappa$ is held fixed, this mean is strictly decreasing in
$D$. If $\kapD=(D\varsigma-1)/(1-\varsigma)$ and $\varsigma>1/D$, then
$\E[\Gamma_{kk}\mid D]=\varsigma$. The implementation floors the shift at
zero: when $\varsigma<1/D$ the row is uniform and the realised mean is
$1/D$, not $\varsigma$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Dirichlet means are concentration ratios, and $\gammaD$ cancels:
$\eta_{kk}/\etadot=(1+\kapD)/(D+\kapD)$. Differentiating in $D$ at fixed
$\kappa$ gives $-(1+\kappa)/(D+\kappa)^2<0$. Solving
$(1+\kappa)/(D+\kappa)=\varsigma$ for $\kappa$ gives
$\kappa=(D\varsigma-1)/(1-\varsigma)$, which is positive exactly when
$\varsigma>1/D$. Uniqueness holds because
$\kappa\mapsto(1+\kappa)/(D+\kappa)$ is strictly monotone on $\kappa>-1$.
\end{proof}

Without the stable shift, the telescoping update would compare candidates
that carry different dynamical priors. At $\kappa=20$ the prior mean stay
is $0.91$ at $D'=3$ and $0.17$ at $D'=101$. That comparison is not the one
the model intends. The code therefore uses \eqref{eq:tilts}.

\section{Closed form after integrating the rows}

The path contributes two count arrays. Every participant's first state is
counted, observed or not, so $n_{\mathrm{init}}=n$:
\begin{equation}
n^{(0)}_d=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\indic{S_{i1}=d},
\qquad
m_{kl}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{t=2}^{T_i}\indic{S_{i,t-1}=k,\,S_{it}=l},
\qquad
m_{k\boldsymbol{\cdot}}=\sum_{l}m_{kl}.
\label{eq:counts}
\end{equation}
Under participant-specific rows the same counts are formed per $i$, written
$m^{(i)}_{kl}$.

\begin{theorem}[Markov path law]
\label{thm:eppf}
Marginalising $\bs\pi^{(0)}$ and $\bGamma$ against \eqref{eq:eta},
\begin{multline}
\Prb(\bs S\mid D)
=
\frac{\Gamma(D\gammaD)}{\Gamma(D\gammaD+n_{\mathrm{init}})}
\prod_{d=1}^{D}\frac{\Gamma(\gammaD+n^{(0)}_d)}{\Gamma(\gammaD)}
\\
\times
\prod_{k=1}^{D}
\Biggl(
\frac{\Gamma(\etadot)}{\Gamma(\etadot+m_{k\boldsymbol{\cdot}})}
\prod_{l=1}^{D}\frac{\Gamma(\eta_{kl}+m_{kl})}{\Gamma(\eta_{kl})}
\Biggr).
\label{eq:eppf}
\end{multline}
Under participant-specific transitions the last double product is replaced
by a sum over $i$ of the same Gamma ratios at $m^{(i)}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The Dirichlet--multinomial integral for a single categorical sample of size
$N$ with counts $\bs n$ and concentration $\bs\alpha$ is
\[
\int_{\simplex^{D-1}}\prod_{j=1}^{D}p_j^{n_j}\,
\Dir(\bs p;\bs\alpha)\,d\bs p
=\frac{B(\bs\alpha+\bs n)}{B(\bs\alpha)}
=\frac{\Gamma(\alpha_{\boldsymbol{\cdot}})}{\Gamma(\alpha_{\boldsymbol{\cdot}}+N)}
\prod_{j}\frac{\Gamma(\alpha_j+n_j)}{\Gamma(\alpha_j)}.
\]
The initial states of the $n$ participants are one such sample against
$\Dir(\gammaD\one_D)$. Each transition row is an independent sample against
$\Dir(\bs\eta_k)$. Multiply the integrals. Relabelling equivariance of
\eqref{eq:eta} (the shift sits on the diagonal, so every permutation of
labels leaves $\bs\eta$ unchanged) makes the same formula available on the
relabelled path.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[What vacant slots contribute]
\label{cor:cancel}
Let state $d$ be empty in $\bs S$, so $n^{(0)}_d=0$ and $m_{dl}=0$ for all
$l$. Then:
\begin{enumerate}
\item its factor in the initial block is
$\Gamma(\gammaD)/\Gamma(\gammaD)=1$;
\item its \emph{row} in the transition block is
$\Gamma(\etadot)/\Gamma(\etadot)\cdot\prod_l\Gamma(\eta_{dl})/\Gamma(\eta_{dl})=1$;
\item its \emph{column} contributes a factor $1$ to every occupied row's
kernel product, but it is not absent from that row: it enters
$\etadot=\sum_{l\le D}\eta_{kl}=\gammaD(D+\kapD)$.
\end{enumerate}
Hence $\Prb(\bs S\mid D)$ depends on $D$ through $\gammaD$, $\kapD$, and
the occupied-row normaliser $\etadot$, and through nothing else.
\end{corollary}

Point (iii) is the whole content of the tilts in the update. Vacant
destinations do not appear as extra Gamma ratios. They appear as a
\emph{known scalar} added to every occupied row's concentration sum. That
scalar is a function of $D$ and $(\alpha_0,\beta,\varsigma)$ only. It does
not depend on a random draw of unused parameters.

Dropping the unit factors, inserting \eqref{eq:eppf} into \eqref{eq:bayes},
and writing $\gamma'=\alpha_0(D')^{-\beta}$,
$\kappa'=\kappa_{D'}$,
$\eta'_{kl}=\gamma'(1+\kappa'\indic{k=l})$,
$\eta'_{\boldsymbol{\cdot}}=\gamma'(D'+\kappa')$
gives the implemented log full conditional, for
$D'\in\{\Dplus,\dots,\Dmax\}$:
\begin{align}
\log\Pi\bigl(D=D'\mid[\bs S]\bigr)
&=
\log p_D(D')+\log D'!-\log(D'-\Dplus)!
\nonumber\\
&\quad
+\log\Gamma(D'\gamma')-\log\Gamma(D'\gamma'+n)
\nonumber\\
&\quad
+\sum_{d=1}^{\Dplus}\bigl[\log\Gamma(\gamma'+n^{(0)}_d)-\log\Gamma(\gamma')\bigr]
\nonumber\\
&\quad
+\sum_{k=1}^{\Dplus}\Bigl[
\log\Gamma(\eta'_{\boldsymbol{\cdot}})
-\log\Gamma(\eta'_{\boldsymbol{\cdot}}+m_{k\boldsymbol{\cdot}})
\Bigr]
\nonumber\\
&\quad
+\sum_{k=1}^{\Dplus}\sum_{l=1}^{\Dplus}
\bigl(\log\Gamma(\eta'_{kl}+m_{kl})-\log\Gamma(\eta'_{kl})\bigr)
+C,
\label{eq:telesc}
\end{align}
with $n=n_{\mathrm{init}}$ and $C$ free of $D'$. This is
\texttt{telescoping\_log\_posterior} in \texttt{R/telescoping.R}. Empty
rows are skipped; columns with zero count contribute
$\log\Gamma(\eta)-\log\Gamma(\eta)=0$, so only the occupied block is
evaluated. Vacant slots still enter through $\eta'_{\boldsymbol{\cdot}}$.

The emission, hurdle included, is absent from \eqref{eq:telesc}. Given
$\bs S$, the observation factors do not depend on $D'$, and a newly created
slot's $(\balpha_d,\brho_d)$ is drawn from the $D$-free base measure, which
integrates to one.

\section{Where softmax breaks}

The multinomial-logit (softmax) specification asked for in this setting is
\begin{equation}
\log\frac{\Gamma^{(i)}_{kl,t}}{\Gamma^{(i)}_{kk,t}}
=\beta^{0}_{kl}+\bs\beta_{kl}^{\top}\bs w_{it}+u_{i,kl},
\qquad
u_{i,kl}\sim\mathcal N(0,\sigma^{2}_{kl}),
\label{eq:softmax}
\end{equation}
with $\eta^{(i)}_{kk,t}=0$ for identifiability and
\begin{equation}
\Gamma^{(i)}_{kl,t}
=\frac{\exp(\eta^{(i)}_{kl,t})}{\sum_{m=1}^{D}\exp(\eta^{(i)}_{km,t})}.
\label{eq:smrow}
\end{equation}
There is nothing wrong with \eqref{eq:softmax} as a model of covariate- or
person-dependent switching. The obstruction is that it cannot be substituted
into \eqref{eq:bayes} while keeping the update that the sampler actually
runs.

Write, for a generic row and a candidate $D'$, the logits
$\bs\eta\in\R^{D'}$ (one coordinate fixed at zero) and the occupied and
vacant index sets $\mathcal O=\{1,\dots,\Dplus\}$,
$\mathcal V=\{\Dplus+1,\dots,D'\}$. Softmax on that row is
\begin{equation}
\Gamma_{l}(\bs\eta)
=\frac{e^{\eta_l}}{Z_{\mathcal O}(\bs\eta)+Z_{\mathcal V}(\bs\eta)},
\qquad
Z_{\mathcal A}(\bs\eta)=\sum_{m\in\mathcal A}e^{\eta_m}.
\label{eq:splitZ}
\end{equation}

\vspace{0.4em}
\begin{theorem}[Obstruction]
\label{thm:obstruct}
Let $\pi_{D'}$ be any prior on the logits of \eqref{eq:smrow} that puts
positive mass on the vacant coordinates $\eta_{\mathcal V}$. Then:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $\Prb(\bs S\mid D')=\displaystyle\int L(\bs S\mid\bs\eta,D')\,\pi_{D'}(d\bs\eta)$
has no Dirichlet--multinomial (Gamma-ratio) closed form.
\item For every occupied destination $l\in\mathcal O$ with $m_{\boldsymbol{\cdot}l}$
not identically zero, $L(\bs S\mid\bs\eta,D')$ depends on $\eta_{\mathcal V}$
through $Z_{\mathcal V}$. Vacant logits do not cancel.
\item Consequently $\Prb(\bs S\mid D')$ is not a function of the occupied
counts, $D'$, and the hyperparameters alone. Equation \eqref{eq:telesc} is
not the full conditional of $D$ under \eqref{eq:softmax}, and there is no
rearrangement of Gamma functions that makes it so.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The complete-data transition factor of one row is
\begin{equation}
\prod_{l=1}^{D'}\Gamma_l^{m_l}
=\exp\Bigl(\sum_{l\in\mathcal O}m_l\eta_l\Bigr)
\bigl(Z_{\mathcal O}(\bs\eta)+Z_{\mathcal V}(\bs\eta)\bigr)^{-m_{\boldsymbol{\cdot}}},
\label{eq:rowlik}
\end{equation}
using $m_l=0$ for $l\in\mathcal V$. The occupied kernel
$\exp(\sum_{l\in\mathcal O}m_l\eta_l)$ does not involve vacant logits. The
normaliser does: any $\eta_v$ for $v\in\mathcal V$ appears as
$e^{\eta_v}$ added to every occupied probability's denominator. Setting
$m_v=0$ does \emph{not} remove $\eta_v$ from the likelihood, because softmax
is a function of the \emph{sum} of the exponentiated logits, not a product
measure on the coordinates.

A Gaussian (or any fully supported density) on $\bs\eta$ therefore leaves
an integral in $\eta_{\mathcal V}$ inside $\Prb(\bs S\mid D')$. That integral
is not a Beta function. The Dirichlet integral is available only when the
prior on the row is Dirichlet on the simplex, whose density
$\prod_l \Gamma_l^{\eta_l-1}/B(\bs\eta)$ is conjugate to
$\prod_l \Gamma_l^{m_l}$ and produces
$B(\bs\eta+\bs m)/B(\bs\eta)$. Softmax-Gaussian and Dirichlet are different
priors on $\simplex^{D'-1}$; conjugacy does not transfer.

For (ii), differentiate \eqref{eq:splitZ} in a vacant logit:
\[
\frac{\partial}{\partial\eta_v}\log\Gamma_l
=-\Gamma_v(\bs\eta)<0
\qquad(l\in\mathcal O,\ v\in\mathcal V).
\]
Every occupied transition probability is strictly decreasing in every vacant
logit. The vacant coordinates are not variation-independent of the occupied
block.

For (iii), the implemented update evaluates \eqref{eq:telesc} at every
$D'$ using only $(n^{(0)},\bs M,\gamma',\kappa')$. Under softmax the
correct integrand still contains $\eta_{\mathcal V}$, a random vector of
length $D'-\Dplus$ redrawn from the prior and, in this project,
$D_{\max}=n+1$. Substituting \eqref{eq:telesc} would compute the
Dirichlet-row posterior, which is a different probability.
\end{proof}

Two further structural mismatches sit underneath the integral.

\paragraph{Dimension.}
After identifiability, the logit vector for one row has length $D'-1$.
Changing $D'$ changes the dimension of the parameter. Dirichlet
concentrations also live in a $D'$-dimensional simplex, but they remain a
\emph{deterministic} function of $D'$ through \eqref{eq:tilts}: moving from
$D'$ to $D'+1$ retunes $\gamma$ and $\kappa$ and adds a known
concentration $\gamma_{D'+1}$ to $\etadot$. Softmax has no such
deterministic padding. Adding a destination multiplies every occupied
probability by the random factor
\begin{equation}
\frac{Z_{\mathcal O}}{Z_{\mathcal O}+e^{\eta_{\mathrm{new}}}}
\in(0,1).
\label{eq:shrink}
\end{equation}
That factor is a draw, not a hyperparameter.

\paragraph{Equivariance.}
The derivation of \eqref{eq:telesc} uses relabelling equivariance:
$\Prb(\bs S\mid D)=\Prb(\sigma(\bs S)\mid D)$ for every permutation
$\sigma$ of $\{1,\dots,D\}$, which holds because $\eta_{\sigma(k)\sigma(l)}=\eta_{kl}$
when the only asymmetry is a diagonal shift. A covariate index, a
baseline destination, or a destination-specific Gaussian random effect
breaks that symmetry. Without it, the combinatorial factor
$D'!/(D'-\Dplus)!$ in \eqref{eq:bayes} is no longer the size of an
equal-probability class.

\section{Consequences}

\subsection*{Dirichlet rows with the two tilts (what the code runs)}

\begin{itemize}
\item $\Prb(\bs S\mid D')$ is \eqref{eq:eppf}, a finite combination of
log-Gamma evaluations on the occupied block.
\item $D\mid[\bs S]$ is a categorical draw on $\{\Dplus,\dots,\Dmax\}$
with acceptance probability one.
\item Vacant slots remain in the model. They affect the posterior of $D$
only through $\gammaD$, $\kapD$, and $\etadot$. Occupied count $\Dplus$
and slot count $D$ stay distinct; $K_{\mathrm{obs}}$ is a third quantity.
\item Participant-specific Dirichlet rows
$\bGamma^{(i)}_k\sim\Dir(\bs\eta_k)$ keep the same closed form, with
counts $m^{(i)}$. Heterogeneity in switching is partial pooling toward
$\bs\eta$, not a logit random effect.
\item Covariates on transitions are given up. Whether a food-group
composition at visit $t$ changes the chance of switching before $t+1$ is
not a parameter of this model.
\item Emissions, including the hurdle $(\rho_{dj},q_{dj})$ with presence
$\rho_{dj}/q_{dj}$, do not inform $D\mid S$. Cardinality is identified
from the dynamics of the path.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Softmax / hierarchical multinomial logit}

\begin{itemize}
\item There is no version of \eqref{eq:telesc} to recover by algebra. The
quantity compared across $D'$ is not a function of the occupied block
alone.
\item A valid unknown-$D$ sampler for \eqref{eq:softmax} would have to
rederive $p(D\mid S)$, typically by reversible-jump or transdimensional
Metropolis--Hastings on $\bs\eta$, with proposals that create and destroy
logit coordinates. That is a different algorithm, with acceptance
probability below one, and it is not implemented.
\item Conditioning on vacant logits rather than integrating them, then
correcting by independence Metropolis, is possible in principle. The
update of $D$ then inherits the mixing of the vacant-parameter block.
Exactness of the present telescoping step is lost.
\item Using \eqref{eq:telesc} while fitting softmax rows samples from the
wrong target: the Dirichlet-tilt posterior on $D$, not the softmax
posterior.
\item Empty-state logits are weakly identified. They enter every occupied
probability through $Z_{\mathcal V}$ and are informed by no transitions
into or out of those states. Telescoping \emph{requires} empty states;
softmax makes them a source of noise in the row normaliser at every
candidate $D'$ up to $\Dmax$.
\item Fitting softmax at a point estimate of $D$ taken from a first-stage
telescoping run is a two-stage procedure that does not propagate
uncertainty in $D$. It is a different inferential claim.
\end{itemize}

\noindent
The telescoping step is a Dirichlet--multinomial calculation with two
$D$-dependent tilts. Softmax replaces that integrand with a non-conjugate
map whose vacant coordinates do not cancel. Those two constructions do not
share a closed-form $p(D\mid S)$.
